# What Does ODRL Mean? A Cross-Level Ontological Grounding of Permissions, Prohibitions, and Duties in UFO-L

- **arXiv:** [2606.24344v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24344) (23 Jun 2026, cs.LO)
- **Benchmark:** https://github.com/Daham-Mustaf/odrl-ufol-grounding

## Core Problem & Motivation

ODRL evaluators emit verdicts (`active`, `violated`, `fulfilled`, `not-set`) but **not** the normative positions a policy brings into existence, the authority those positions presuppose, or **who may declare a violation**.

Motivating scenario (Datenraum Kultur): KulturPortal holds two Agreements prohibiting `distribute` of showtimes—BerlinerEnsemble remedy `compensate`, DeutschesTheater remedy `delete`. Evaluator reports `violated` twice, but Formal Semantics **defines no evaluation procedure for remedy duties**; Evaluation Report unspecified. Cannot answer (i) by whose standing a remedy is demanded, (ii) what grounds the remedy.

Four manifestations:

| ID | Problem |
|---|---|
| **P1** | `behaviour` open vs closed creates **ontologically incompatible** positions; no account of which exist |
| **P2** | Violation-declaration authority delegated to implementors → incompatible runtimes |
| **P3** | Every prohibition entails assigner’s **Right to Omission**; neither graph nor evaluator represents it |
| **P4** | No vocabulary to declare sanctioning authority at authoring time |

**This paper does not give ODRL a denotational semantics**; it answers *what exists when a policy is in force* via UFO-L.

## Architecture / Formalism

### ODRL recap used

\(\pi = \langle P, F, D \rangle\), rule \(r = \langle a, x, y, t, C \rangle\): action **type** (not token), target **token**, parties tokens (Agreement) or roles (Offer). **Offers must not be evaluated**; **Sets create no binding bond** (out of grounding scope). Duties in four roles: `obligation`, `duty`, `remedy`, `consequence` (cascade in IM; Formal Semantics has **no activation procedure**). Conflict property: `perm` / `prohibit` / `invalid` (default). Formal Semantics `behaviour`: **open** = silence permits; **closed** = default, silence prohibits.

### UFO-L

Legal positions as moments in simple legal relators (exactly one correlative pair). Conduct: Permission–No Right, Right–Duty. Competence: Power–Subjection, Immunity–Disability.

### Deontic analysis

**Permission underspecified (weak vs strong).** Strong permission needs Immunity against later prohibition. Under `behaviour=open`, undeclared actions carry **no positive position** (runtime convention, not a granted Permission)—**Proposition 5.1 Open-World Normative Vacuum**. Under `closed`, each declared permission founds Permission–No Right; that is **consistent with but not equivalent to** strong permission (no persistence protection in Formal Semantics).

**Proposition 3.1 — Prohibition is sanctioned, not regimented.** Proof: if regimented, `odrl:remedy` would be unreachable, contradicting the Recommendation. Hence violation is possible and consequential → needs Power–Subjection for violation-to-remedy.

**Proposition 3.2 — Formal Semantics covers achievement obligations only.** Three-state (fulfilled / violated / not-set) presupposes an event-bounded satisfier. Maintenance and process obligations (Dam et al. 22 patterns) have no transition.

### Cross-Level Design Principle

Axioms A1–A3: state changes need an institutional event; events need a competent agent; competence is Power–Subjection.

- Weak permission is **conduct-level-complete** (Prop. 4.1).
- Strong permission, sanctioned prohibition, and any violable+consequential norm are **cross-level** (Thm 4.1–4.3).
- **Principle 1:** languages with violable consequential norms **must** provide competence-level positions; conduct-only vocabularies cannot ground violation→consequence.

### Relators (Definition 5.1) and FOL axioms 5.1–5.11

| ODRL rule | Relator | Positions (assignee / assigner) |
|---|---|---|
| Permission (weak) | \(\rho_P\) | Permission to Act / No Right |
| Permission (strong) † | \(\rho_P + \rho_I\) | + Immunity / Disability |
| Prohibition | \(\rho_F\) | Duty to Omit \(rfr(a)\) / Right to Omission |
| Prohibition + remedy † | \(\rho_F + \rho_R\) | + Power(\(y\), \(decl(a)\)) / Subjection(\(x\), \(decl(a)\)) |
| Duty | \(\rho_D\) | Duty to Act / Right to Action |

\(rfr(a)\) = forbearance (omission perdurant); \(decl(a)\) = institutional declare-violation act. Distinct founding predicates keep conduct vs competence relators separate (Ax 5.6 unique founding). Correlativity (Ax 5.9): exactly one of each pair. **Ax 5.10:** Permission to Act and Duty to Omit over same \(\langle a,t\rangle\) for same bearer → ⊥. **Ax 5.11:** Disability of assigner precludes creating a prohibition.

**Prop 5.2 Evaluator soundness & incompleteness:** under closed, evaluator-permits ⇒ Permission in \(\mathrm{Pos}(\pi,e)\); prohibition ⇒ Duty to Omit; prohibition+remedy ⇒ Power. Converse fails: four of eight positions absent from Evaluation Report.

**odrl-l: profile:** 15 classes, 8 properties; extension points `odrl-l:hasRemedy`, `odrl-l:stronglyPermitted`. OWL limits: no function symbols so \(rfr(a)\) unrepresentable; `stronglyPermitted` is an annotation (no OWL entailment of \(\rho_I\)); Ax 5.9–5.10 need full FOL.

## Evaluation & Benchmarks

Axioms encoded in **TPTP/FOF** (Vampire 5.0.0, E 3.2.5), **SMT-LIB 2 UF** (Z3 4.15.4), and **Isabelle/HOL** (all axioms, corollary, sanctioned-prohibition lifecycle, Prop. 5.2).

**39 problems** over 36 IDs (GRND001–036), three with two variants (open/closed, sanctioned/regimented, with/without immunity). Each: TPTP + SMT-LIB + Turtle over DRK entities. Entailment vs discriminating problems.

- Full axiom set **satisfiable** (Vampire finite-model: one Permission, two agents).
- Table 2: perm-relator-weak Thm; strong Thm/Uns; proh conduct Thm/Uns; remedy Thm; obl Thm/Sat; unique-founding ×6 Thm; correlativity ×4 Thm/Uns; Ax 5.10 Uns; disability-block Uns; A1–A3 Thm; consistency Sat.
- **E omits 3 satisfiable problems** (no finite-model finder); **Z3 times out** on those in UF; Vampire portfolio discharges them.
- Motivating scenario: activating BerlinerEnsemble prohibition derives the Power licensing violation declaration.

## Limitations & Failure Modes

- **Simple legal relationships only**; Liberty relator and richer Hohfeldian structures out of scope.
- Offers/Sets not grounded; Formal Semantics already forbids evaluating Offers.
- Remedy *evaluation procedure* still missing from W3C draft—this paper grounds *authority*, not the operational cascade.
- OWL profile is an **approximation**; runtime conflict of Permission vs Duty to Omit needs FOL/SMT, not OWL.
- Maintenance/process duties remain unevaluable.
- `inheritFrom` conflict detection listed as future work.
- Connection to LLM generation is future work (positions as extraction targets).

## Key Takeaways for an NL-to-ODRL system

1. Treat Formal Semantics **verdicts as incomplete**. After generation, instantiate `Pos(π,e)`: eight positions, not two.
2. **Never treat `behaviour=open` as “everyone has Permission.”** It is a normative vacuum (Prop. 5.1). Closed-world permissions are still **weak** unless `odrl-l:stronglyPermitted`.
3. Every prohibition with `remedy` must record **who holds Power to declare violation** (assigner by default; allow delegation). Two providers ⇒ two Powers.
4. Surface **Right to Omission / Right to Action** for assigners so remedy selection has a claimant.
5. Use Ax 5.10 as a **hard conflict check** in SMT: Permission(x,a,t) ∧ Duty(x,rfr(a),t) unsat.
6. Generation targets should include `odrl-l:` terms, not only core ODRL.
7. Achievement-only duty semantics: NL that says “keep data encrypted” (maintenance) **cannot** be faithfully compiled to current Formal Semantics.
